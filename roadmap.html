learning path

Hardware hacking roadmap

This path starts slow on purpose. The goal is to understand voltage, protocols, and firmware before trying advanced exploitation work.

1

10 percent

Electronics Basics

Learn voltage, current, resistance, pull-ups, breadboards, and why ground is not optional.

Skills: Ohm's law, multimeter use, reading simple schematics

2

24 percent

Microcontrollers

Use Arduino, Pico, or ESP32 to control pins, read sensors, blink LEDs, and debug basic code.

Skills: GPIO, PWM, interrupts, serial output

3

38 percent

Communication Protocols

Capture and decode UART, SPI, I2C, and basic USB behavior with a logic analyzer.

Skills: bus sniffing, baud rates, timing, datasheets

4

52 percent

Embedded Systems

Understand bootloaders, memory maps, debug ports, firmware updates, and device constraints.

Skills: flashing firmware, SWD/JTAG basics, logs

5

67 percent

Wireless Security

Study WiFi, BLE, and sub-GHz behavior using lab networks, test devices, and known-good captures.

Skills: radio basics, advertisements, channels, safe test plans

6

82 percent

Hardware Exploitation

Move into firmware extraction, insecure debug ports, side-channel awareness, and fault concepts.

Skills: threat modeling, firmware triage, hardware notes

7

100 percent

Advanced Red Team Hardware

Practice reporting, payload safety, implant detection, and controlled assessments with explicit scope.

Skills: scoping, evidence, defensive validation, cleanup

pace note

Slow labs beat random tool collecting.

Spend a week at each stage if you are new. Keep a lab notebook with wiring photos, screenshots, and what failed. The boring notes become the useful ones later.
